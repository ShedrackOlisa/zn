Yes, this is a God of War clone. I'll say that upfront because it's obvious within the first ten minutes - a returning crusader wielding a giant weapon, ripping enemies apart with brutal finishers, climbing through a mythological underworld instead of Greek mythology. But once I let go of the comparison, Dante's Inferno turned out to be one of the most visually unhinged action games on PSP. Dragging Dante down through all Nine Circles of Hell, fighting demons made of literal sin, is a wild enough hook to carry the derivative combat on its own.

Loosely adapted from Dante Alighieri's Inferno, the game reimagines the poet as a crusader returning from war to find his beloved Beatrice murdered and dragged to Hell, so he descends after her, cross-shaped scythe in hand, cutting through each circle of the underworld in turn. It's pulpy and takes enormous liberties with the source material, but that's part of the appeal - watching a piece of 14th-century literature get turned into a gore-soaked action game is a strange kind of fun on its own. Combat: Familiar, But It Swings Well

The moveset will feel instantly recognizable if you've played God of War - light and heavy scythe attacks, a "holy" and "unholy" magic system that functions as this game's version of Kratos's dual weapon options, and satisfying, gory finishing moves on weakened enemies. It's not innovative, and it doesn't try particularly hard to be, but the fundamentals are executed competently: the scythe has real weight behind it, unholy magic lets you crowd-control groups of smaller demons, and holy magic gives you options against tougher single targets. It's comfort-food combat, but it's well-made comfort food.

Each Circle Of Hell Has Its Own Twisted Identity

This is where the game genuinely distinguishes itself from its inspiration. Every circle of Hell is themed around its associated sin and pushed to a genuinely unsettling visual extreme - Lust isn't depicted as seductive so much as grotesque and disturbing, Gluttony is a rancid nightmare of rot and excess, and Greed reimagines gold as piles of screaming, living coins rather than simple treasure. The art direction throughout is consistently, deliberately upsetting in a way that makes each new circle feel like a genuine escalation rather than a reskinned level.

Boss Fights Built From Historical And Mythological Figures

The boss roster leans into figures pulled from history and mythology reinterpreted as monstrous demons, and the fights are memorable largely because of how grotesque and over-the-top their designs get. These encounters lean on scale and spectacle over mechanical complexity, but as set pieces they're some of the most visually striking moments in the game, and they give the campaign a series of genuine highlights to build toward rather than letting the pacing go flat between regular enemy encounters.

Absolution And Judgment: A Small But Interesting Twist

Layered on top of the hack-and-slash combat is a light morality system: defeated souls can be absolved (granting experience toward holy magic) or damned (granting experience toward unholy magic and, cynically, more points). It's not a deep branching-choice system, but it gives you a reason to think about your build rather than just mashing attack, and it ties thematically into the game's whole "judging sinners" framing in a way that feels more purposeful than a bolted-on mechanic usually would.

A Few Things I'd Tell A Friend Before They Download:
• Pick either holy or unholy magic to invest in early rather than splitting resources evenly.
• Absolving or damning souls affects your magic progression, so be consistent with your choice.
• Don't go in expecting mechanical innovation - this is God of War's formula, and it knows it.
• The imagery gets genuinely disturbing in places, so maybe don't play right before bed.
• Boss fights lean on spectacle, so enjoy the visual absurdity rather than expecting deep mechanics.

Performance And The Honest Limitations

Running through PPSSPP, this performs smoothly on virtually any modern Android device, with the gore effects and particle-heavy combat holding up without noticeable slowdown. That said, the combat's biggest weakness is exactly what you'd expect from a clone - it lacks the depth and polish God of War's own combat systems eventually built toward, and later encounters can start to feel repetitive if you're relying purely on the base moveset. The story also takes significant creative liberties with its source material that purists of the actual poem may find frustrating rather than fun.

Is Dante's Inferno Worth Downloading?

If you can accept it for what it is - a shameless but well-crafted God of War clone dressed up in genuinely disturbing, memorable underworld imagery - Dante's Inferno delivers a satisfying, brutal action romp. The combat won't reinvent anything, but the atmosphere, boss designs, and sheer commitment to making Hell feel appropriately awful carry it a long way. For fans of dark, over-the-top hack-and-slash action, it's a compelling handheld option that's absolutely worth the download.
