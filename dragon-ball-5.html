A Roster That Reaches Into Dragon Ball Super

Where the fourth entry focused on padding out the classic Z-Fighter lineup, this build reaches further into the timeline, bringing in fighters and forms associated with Dragon Ball Super alongside the expected roster of Goku, Vegeta, and company. It's an ambitious move for a fan project built on aging PSP-era tech, and the integration isn't flawless - some newer additions clearly required more creative workarounds to fit the engine's animation systems than others - but the sheer scope of the attempt is impressive on its own.

Tournament Mode Gives Single-Player A Real Arc

Rather than just throwing you into isolated matches, this entry structures single-player around an actual tournament bracket, complete with escalating difficulty as you advance toward a final bout. It's a small structural change, but it makes solo play feel like it's building toward something, rather than just being a string of disconnected fights against the CPU. Difficulty scaling within the bracket feels reasonably tuned too, ramping up steadily rather than throwing an unfair spike at you in an early round.

Beam Struggles And Finishers Still Carry The Combat

The dramatic centerpiece of any Budokai-style fight - two fighters locked in a beam clash, mashing to overpower the other - remains just as tense here as in the earlier games, and finishing sequences lean into the series' love of over-the-top, screen-filling energy attacks. It's not a technical overhaul of the combat engine, but the fan team behind this clearly understood that spectacle is half of what makes these fights memorable, and they leaned into that rather than trying to reinvent the underlying mechanics.

A Fan Project With Its Own Identity, Not Just A Reskin

It would have been easy for this to just be Shin Budokai 4 with a few more characters bolted on, but the tournament structure and Super-era roster additions give this entry a genuinely different feel and focus. That said, it's still a fan-made build rather than an official release, so expect some inconsistency in voice work and animation polish across the newer additions compared to the core cast. For a project built without a studio's resources, though, the ambition on display here is hard not to respect.

Performance And The Honest Limitations

This runs smoothly through PPSSPP on virtually any modern Android device, since the underlying engine was built for original PSP hardware and doesn't push demanding requirements. None of that derails the experience, but it's worth going in with realistic expectations about a passion project rather than a polished commercial release.

Is Dragon Ball Z Shin Budokai 5 Worth Downloading?

It's not a polished official release, and a few roster additions show their fan-made seams, but the ambition and genuine care behind the project shine through. If you want a structured, tournament-driven DBZ fighting experience on PPSSPP, this is a great pick.
