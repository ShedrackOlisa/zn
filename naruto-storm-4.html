The biggest roster the series has ever had

This is where the Storm series throws restraint out the window - Naruto alone comes in base, Sage Mode, Kurama Chakra Mode, and Six Paths variants, Sasuke spans Hebi through his Rinnegan-era form, and the supporting cast reaches deep into the War arc's combatants including Madara, Obito, Hashirama, and every reanimated Kage worth naming. The PSP port keeps the full roster intact even if individual character models take a visual hit, and the movesets stay faithful to what each form is known for.

Combat built on movement and reads

Chakra dashes close distance fast, substitution jutsu let you punish overcommitted attacks by teleporting behind an opponent, and awakening mode transforms your fighter into a stronger version once your gauge fills - Naruto into Kurama Mode, Sasuke into Susanoo. It's a system that rewards spacing and timing over raw combo memorization, which keeps matches readable even at high speed. The PSP conversion simplifies some inputs, but the core rock-paper-scissors of dash, substitute, and punish survives the trip.

Story mode adapts the war arc's biggest moments

The campaign follows the Fourth Great Ninja War beat for beat, putting you in control of Naruto, Sasuke, Kakashi, and the assembled Kage as the story escalates from clone-army skirmishes to the fight against Madara, Obito, and eventually Kaguya. Cutscenes are compressed compared to console but the pacing and dialogue land the same emotional beats, and the boss fights against Six Paths-era antagonists still feel suitably climactic even scaled down for a portable screen.

Ad-hoc multiplayer keeps rivalries alive

Local ad-hoc versus matches are supported for players who want to settle Naruto-versus-Sasuke arguments the old-fashioned way, and the netcode holds up fine at close range. It won't replace online play, but as a couch-multiplayer anime fighter it does the job, and team battle modes give it more structure than a simple 1v1 ladder. This is one of the few PSP-era Naruto conversions where local multiplayer feels like a genuine feature rather than an afterthought.

Things I Wish Someone Had Told Me Earlier:
• Learn substitution jutsu timing before anything else - it's the single most important defensive tool in the game.
• Chakra dash spam works better as an approach tool than a combo extender; use it to close distance safely.
• Awakening mode transformations are strongest as comeback tools - don't waste the meter early in a match.
• Ultimate jutsu deal huge damage but leave you exposed on whiff - punish openings, don't force them.
• If frame rate dips during team battles or big cutscenes, drop rendering resolution to 1x PSP first.
• A controller is close to mandatory here - the input list is dense and touchscreen play blunts a lot of the combat's nuance.
