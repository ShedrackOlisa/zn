This is the Prince of Persia entry people forget exists, and that's a shame. Slotted in canonically between Sands of Time and Warrior Within, Forgotten Sands doesn't reinvent the trilogy so much as perfect its acrobatics one more time before the series changed direction. On PPSSPP it holds up remarkably well - the wall-runs still feel weightless, the rewind power still bails you out of the platforming mistake you just made, and army-summoning turns you into a one-Prince siege engine for a few glorious seconds. If you loved the original Sands of Time but wanted tighter combat and bigger set pieces, this quietly delivers both.

The setup is simple - the Prince stumbles into his brother's kingdom just as it's being overrun by an ancient sand army, and ends up bonding with a genie-like spirit who hands him time-rewind and elemental powers to fight back. It's a bit of a retcon-heavy detour from the main trilogy's timeline, and the story is easily the weakest part of the package. What matters is that the acrobatics and combat built around that story are consistently excellent, and the compressed ISO here keeps every bit of that intact without demanding much from your device.

Rewind Power: Still The Best Safety Net In Platforming

Missed a leap over a spike pit? Rewind three seconds and try again without losing a shred of momentum or a life. It sounds like it should make the platforming trivial, but the level designers clearly built encounters knowing you'd lean on it - jumps get longer, timing windows get tighter, and puzzle rooms start chaining two or three environmental effects together. The result is a game that's forgiving in the moment but still demanding overall, which is exactly the balance the original Sands of Time nailed and this one manages to match.

Summoning An Army Mid-Fight Never Stops Being Satisfying

Combat here leans harder into spectacle than the first game did. Freeze an enemy solid and shatter them, or pop your army-summon ability and watch a squad of sand warriors pour out of the ground to help you clear a room. It's flashier than the stealth-and-precision combat from Sands of Time, closer in spirit to what Warrior Within would push further, and it works as a nice bridge between the two games' very different tones.

Elemental Puzzles Add Real Variety To The Exploration

Beyond combat, the elemental powers reshape how you move through the ruins - freezing a waterfall mid-fall turns it into a climbable wall, and igniting oil-soaked rope opens up entirely new routes. These aren't throwaway gimmicks either; several of the game's best vertical set pieces exist purely because of them, and figuring out which power unlocks which path is consistently the most satisfying puzzle-solving in the whole trilogy.

Tips Before You Jump In:
• Don't hoard rewind charges - the game refills them generously, and hesitating to use one usually costs you more time than spending it.
• Set "Rendering Resolution" to 2x PSP in PPSSPP; the sand and water elemental effects look noticeably better at that setting.
• Freeze water mid-jump rather than before you leap - timing it in the air gives you more platforming options.
• Save your army-summon for rooms with multiple enemies rather than single tough fights; it's far more cost-effective there.

Performance On PPSSPP

This is one of the lighter PSP-era action games to emulate, and it shows. Frame pacing stays smooth even during the busier elemental set pieces, and the compressed ISO doesn't noticeably hurt the sandstorm and water effects that make the visuals pop. Load times between checkpoints are quick, which matters given how often the rewind mechanic has you retrying short platforming bursts.

Is Prince Of Persia Forgotten Sands Worth Your Time?

If you've already played Sands of Time and Warrior Within and skipped this one, it's worth circling back to - the acrobatics are as good as the trilogy gets, even if the story feels bolted on. If you're new to the series entirely, it's actually a gentler, more forgiving entry point than either of its neighbors thanks to the rewind mechanic. Either way, this compressed ISO is a great way to experience a genuinely underrated chapter of the franchise.
