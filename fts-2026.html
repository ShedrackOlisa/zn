A Scouting System That Rewards Patience

The biggest surprise in FTS 26 is how seriously it treats squad building. Instead of just handing you a transfer list, there's an actual scouting layer where you send staff to look at prospects and get graded reports back before committing to a signing. Find a raw 17-year-old with hidden potential and develop him into a first-team regular over a few seasons, and the payoff feels earned rather than scripted. The mod removes the usual grind around unlocking top-tier players and formations, so you can experiment with your ideal tactical setup from day one instead of waiting seasons to afford it.

Create-a-Player Goes Deeper Than It Needs To

You can build a fully custom player, or an entire custom squad, down to appearance, preferred foot, and celebration style, then drop that squad into any league in the game. I recreated a Sunday league side and watched them get demolished by a top-flight club, and it was still more entertaining than most default career starts. This depth of customization is unusual for a game this lightweight, and it's the feature most likely to keep you coming back long after the novelty of the core matches wears off.

On-Pitch Play: Tight, Not Simplistic

Match play strikes a real balance between arcade accessibility and mechanical depth. Passing needs actual aim rather than a lock-on assist, shot timing genuinely matters, too early and you skew wide, too late and it's blocked, and skill moves like step-overs and feints are responsive without feeling gimmicky. It's faster-paced than a full simulation but slower and more deliberate than pure arcade football, landing in a sweet spot where matches stay exciting without feeling weightless. Every goal here feels like it came from a decision you made, not a script the game ran for you.

Where The Depth Runs Out

To be fair about it: this isn't Football Manager, and the deeper you push into the management side, the more you'll notice the ceiling. Contract negotiations are simplified compared to a true management sim, staff roles are limited to a handful of categories, and the simulation engine for matches you don't play manually is serviceable but not smart, you'll win more games playing them out yourself than letting the AI simulate results. If you're coming from a heavier management title, treat FTS 26's depth as a pleasant bonus on top of an arcade game, not a replacement for the real thing.
