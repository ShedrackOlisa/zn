A roster that actually feels like early Shippuden

The character select screen here is stacked with the cast fans wanted right at the start of the timeskip - Naruto in his new outfit, Sakura post-training, Kakashi, Sai, Yamato, Sasori, Deidara, and a healthy chunk of the Akatsuki alongside classic Leaf ninja. Each fighter carries a distinct moveset built around their actual jutsu rather than generic punches reskinned with chakra effects, and the support-character system lets you call in a second fighter mid-match for combo extensions or clutch saves.

Story mode retells the arcs without dragging

Rather than a loosely-connected string of fights, the story mode threads together cutscenes and battles that actually track with the manga's early Shippuden arcs, giving context to why you're fighting Sasori or teaming up against Akatsuki scouts. It's not going to replace watching the anime, but it hits the beats efficiently and gives every unlockable fight a reason to exist, which a lot of anime fighters skip entirely in favor of a bare-bones arcade ladder.

Ultimate jutsu cinematics are still a highlight

Landing a finishing blow at the right moment triggers a fully animated cutscene of your character's signature technique - Naruto's Rasengan barrage, Sasuke's Chidori, Sakura's chakra-enhanced punch sending someone flying through three walls. These sequences are clearly where the development budget went, and even compressed for PSP they land with real impact. It's the kind of spectacle that makes casual matches feel like actual anime moments instead of generic fighting-game finishers.

Where the age shows

This is an older-generation anime fighter, and it plays like one - hitboxes are looser than modern fighting games, some support jutsu feel overtuned compared to others, and the AI on higher difficulties leans on cheap punishes rather than smart play. None of that ruins the experience, but go in expecting a game built around fun chaos over competitive balance. If you want tournament-grade fighting mechanics, look elsewhere; if you want to feel like you're in a Naruto fight scene, this delivers.

Things I Wish Someone Had Told Me Earlier:
• Pick support characters that cover your main's weaknesses, not just your favorites - it changes matches more than you'd expect.
• Ultimate jutsu inputs are more forgiving than they look; don't be afraid to go for them mid-combo.
• Story mode unlocks characters faster than grinding versus matches, so play through it first.
• Higher AI difficulties punish button-mashing hard - block more than you attack against them.
• Enable frame skipping if jutsu cutscenes stutter on lower-end devices.
• Some Akatsuki characters unlock through hidden story-mode conditions, not just completion.

Is Naruto Shippuden: Ultimate Ninja Heroes 2 Worth Playing?

Yes, especially if you want something snappier and less mechanically dense than the Storm series for quick portable sessions. The roster is generous, the ultimate jutsu cinematics still deliver, and the story mode gives the early Shippuden arcs a genuine sense of stakes. It's an older-feeling fighter with looser mechanics than modern anime games, but at around 700MB and with matches this quick to jump into, it's an easy recommendation for anyone who wants classic Naruto fighting-game energy on their phone.
