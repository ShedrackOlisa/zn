Same Los Santos, Very Different Toolkit

The map is still San Andreas' original Los Santos, San Fierro, and Las Venturas, no new areas, no rebuilt geography. What's changed is how you move through it. Weapon switching uses a proper radial wheel instead of cycling one at a time, which alone makes firefights feel dramatically less clunky than vanilla SA ever did. The HUD is redesigned to mimic GTA V's minimap and health/armor display, and the wanted-level indicator borrows V's star styling. None of this touches the underlying map data, it's a gameplay and interface layer sitting on top of a game you already know by heart.

Custom Missions Built To Capture V's Tone

Beyond the original CJ campaign, the mod adds a set of bonus missions written specifically to feel like something out of GTA V rather than San Andreas. Expect heist-flavored setups, coordinated multi-stage jobs, and a Franklin-inspired playable character who gets a special driving ability similar to V's slow-motion car control. These aren't reskinned versions of existing SA missions, they're new content built around the borrowed mechanics, which is the most impressive part of the whole package. They're not going to be mistaken for Rockstar's own writing, but as fan-made bonus content, they're a genuine addition rather than filler.

The Stock Market And Other Small Systems

One of the more unexpected additions is a stripped-down stock market you can check and invest in between missions, a nod to GTA V's Lifeinvader/BAWSAQ systems rather than a full simulation. It's simple, a handful of stocks that move based on story triggers, but it gives idle money somewhere to go beyond just buying weapons and safehouses. It's a small system, not deep enough to build a strategy around, but it's a thoughtful touch that shows the modders were thinking about V's broader systems, not just its combat feel.

Visuals Get A Pass Too, Just Not The Main Event

There's a visual layer here as well, though it takes a back seat to the gameplay changes. Some textures are sharpened, lighting gets a modest boost, and a few recognizable GTA V landmarks (a Vinewood-style sign, a Maze Bank-inspired tower silhouette) are worked into the skyline as visual nods rather than full recreations. If you want a dedicated ground-up visual overhaul of San Andreas, that's a different mod entirely, this one spends its effort on how the game plays, with the graphics update functioning more as a complementary bonus than the headline feature.

A Few Things I'd Tell A Friend Before They Download:
• Play through at least a few original CJ missions first to see how the new HUD and weapon wheel actually feel in context.
• Try the bonus heist-style missions early, they're the best showcase of what this mod adds.
• Don't expect Rockstar-quality voice acting or writing in the custom missions, judge them as fan content.
• The stock market system is minor, don't build your whole playthrough economy around it.
• If a custom mission bugs out, reload rather than force-quitting, some triggers are timing-sensitive.

Performance And Honest Limitations

Running two games' worth of systems on top of each other has a cost. The mod is noticeably heavier than vanilla San Andreas, and older or budget phones will see more stutter, particularly during the custom heist missions with more moving parts on screen. A few of the bonus missions have rough scripting, the occasional objective marker doesn't update correctly, or an NPC gets stuck. None of it is game-breaking, but it's clearly fan-made rather than QA-tested software, and patience helps when something doesn't behave as expected.

Is GTA San Andreas Mod GTA 5 Worth Downloading?

If you love San Andreas but wish it played a bit more like V, yes, this is a clever and genuinely ambitious hybrid worth trying. The weapon wheel and HUD changes alone make combat feel more modern, and the custom heist-style missions add real content rather than just padding. It's not going to replace either game, and the visual side is a secondary feature rather than the draw. But as an experiment in blending two GTA eras into one mobile package, it's one of the more interesting mods in this space, rough edges and all.
