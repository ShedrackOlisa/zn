DC RPG Co-op
Before Arkham Asylum redefined what a superhero game could be, there was this: a scrappy, beat-em-up action RPG that let you actually play as the whole Justice League roster at once. Justice League Heroes on PSP takes cues from X-Men Legends' co-op dungeon-crawler formula and reskins it with DC's biggest names, Superman, Batman, Wonder Woman, The Flash, and more. I went in expecting a forgettable licensed cash-in riding the movie hype train. Instead I found a genuinely well-constructed hack-and-slash with a surprisingly solid story credit behind it and a gameplay loop that's stayed fun far longer than it has any right to.

The structure is straightforward: pick two League members from a roster that includes the heaviest hitters in DC's stable, then smash your way through levels, leveling up abilities and unlocking new costumes and powers along the way. It's built explicitly around switching between characters mid-fight to combine their strengths, and that switching mechanic is where most of the game's fun actually lives. The story, penned by acclaimed comics writer Dwayne McDuffie, holds up as a genuinely solid League tale pitting the team against Brainiac and Darkseid, giving the game a narrative backbone a lot of licensed superhero titles never bother with.

Mixing And Matching Powers Is The Whole Point

The real appeal here isn't any single character, it's the combinations. Batman can sneak into position and lay down gadget-based traps while Superman soars overhead unleashing heat vision to soften up a crowd. The Flash zips around the battlefield at speed, launching enemies into juggle combos that a slower, heavier character like Martian Manhunter can then finish off with a devastating follow-up. Switching between your two active heroes mid-encounter to chain these interactions together is genuinely satisfying, and it gives what could have been a repetitive button-masher real tactical texture.

A Story That Actually Respects The Characters

It would have been easy to phone in a paper-thin plot for a licensed action game like this, but Dwayne McDuffie's writing credit shows. The story against Brainiac and Darkseid feels like something you'd find in an actual Justice League comic run, with character voices that stay true to how these heroes are typically written rather than generic action-hero dialogue slapped onto recognizable faces. It's not going to be remembered as a landmark superhero narrative, but it's a cut above what most games in this genre bothered to attempt, and it gives the campaign enough forward momentum to want to see through to the end.

Leveling Up And Unlocking Costumes Adds Real Replay Value

Each hero has their own ability tree to invest in as you progress, letting you specialize a character toward crowd control, single-target damage, or support utility depending on your preferred playstyle. Alternate costumes unlock as you play, some purely cosmetic, others with minor stat tweaks that reward experimenting with different heroes rather than sticking to one favorite pairing the whole game. Because the roster is large enough to support several genuinely different two-character combinations, there's real incentive to replay levels with a different team and see how the encounter design changes when your available toolkit does.

Co-op Makes This Even Better With A Friend

While perfectly enjoyable solo, this game was clearly built with a co-op partner in mind, and playing it that way changes the dynamic considerably. Splitting the League's roster between two players and coordinating who's tanking, who's controlling crowds, and who's dealing the finishing blows turns individual levels into something closer to a small-scale raid encounter than a solo dungeon crawl. On PPSSPP, getting co-op working requires a bit of setup through ad-hoc emulation, but for anyone with a friend who also grew up on DC comics, it's worth the extra effort to set up.

Performance And What's Rough Around The Edges

This runs comfortably on PPSSPP across most modern Android phones without demanding aggressive settings tweaks, a benefit of being a PSP-era title with fairly modest technical requirements. The rough edges are mostly cosmetic, environments repeat more than a bigger-budget game would allow, and animations for some of the less iconic League members feel noticeably less polished than the marquee characters. Combat occasionally devolves into camera confusion when several enemies surround you at once, a common issue in beat-em-ups of this era. None of it undermines the core loop, but it's worth knowing going in that this is a solid B-tier action game, not a AAA production.

Is Justice League Heroes Worth Playing In 2026?

If you're a DC fan who's never given this one a chance, yes, it's an underrated gem that deserves more attention than it typically gets. The character-switching combat holds up well, the McDuffie-penned story is genuinely engaging by licensed-game standards, and the roster variety gives it real replay value most beat-em-ups lack. It's not going to compete with the Arkham series for polish, but as a co-op-friendly dungeon crawler that makes you feel like an actual member of the World's Greatest Superheroes, it still delivers a good time.
