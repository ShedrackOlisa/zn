Okay, let's be real for a second: if you grew up watching Ben 10, you've absolutely imagined slapping the Omnitrix and turning into Four Arms to smash something. The Omnitrix Roster: Simple Kits, Big Personality

Each alien plays like a distinct toy rather than a stat variant of the last one. Four Arms is your straightforward heavy hitter, tossing enemies around with brute strength; Heatblast trades range for chip damage with sustained fireballs; Diamondhead turns the whole fight into crystal-shard chaos against robotic enemies; and XLR8 is pure speed, letting you zip past danger or suplex enemies before they know what hit them. None of these movesets are mechanically deep on their own, but the game leans on swapping between them mid-level to keep encounters from feeling repetitive, and it mostly succeeds.

Vilgax And The Forever Knights: Familiar Faces, Faithful Fights

The story doesn't reinvent anything from the show, and it doesn't need to - Vilgax's menace and the Forever Knights' medieval-tech aesthetic translate well into boss encounters and mid-level skirmishes. Boss fights specifically ask you to think about which alien counters which attack pattern, which is about as much strategic depth as the combat gets, but it's enough to keep the brawling from turning into mindless button mashing. Longtime fans will recognize plenty of small nods to specific episodes sprinkled through the level design and dialogue.

A Faithful Console Port, Not A Watered-Down Mobile Cut

One of the pleasant surprises with this PSP release is how little was trimmed compared to what console-era Ben 10 games offered around the same time. The level count, alien roster, and boss encounters all feel complete rather than truncated for a handheld release. Running through PPSSPP on a modern phone, the compressed ISO holds onto that completeness while adding the benefit of higher resolution rendering than the original hardware could manage, so the cel-shaded art style actually looks cleaner than it did on an actual PSP screen.

Puzzle-Light, Combat-Heavy Pacing

Don't go in expecting real puzzle design - the environmental puzzles here are more about picking the right alien for a specific obstacle (melt ice as Heatblast, squeeze through a gap as XLR8) than anything that will make you stop and think. That's by design: this is a combat-first game meant for quick pick-up-and-play sessions, and the puzzle beats exist mainly to pace out the brawling and give each alien a reason to exist outside of combat. If you came here for Metroidvania-style puzzle depth, look elsewhere; if you came here to smash things as a fire-breathing alien, it delivers exactly that.

Performance And The Honest Limitations

Running through PPSSPP, this holds a stable frame rate on virtually any modern Android device - it's a lightweight PSP-era title that doesn't push hardware limits. The trade-off is that the combat, while fun, genuinely is shallow if you're looking for mechanical depth; there's no combo system to master, and most fights come down to picking the right alien rather than executing complex inputs. The campaign is also on the shorter side, more suited to nostalgic afternoons than a long-term single-player commitment. None of that undercuts what it's trying to be, but it's worth knowing going in.

Is Ben 10: Protector Of Earth Worth Downloading?

If you grew up on the show, this is an easy recommendation - it captures the humor, the character voices, and the alien-swapping power fantasy better than most licensed games manage. Even without the nostalgia, it's a solid pick-up-and-play brawler that doesn't overstay its welcome or take itself too seriously. It won't challenge you, and it won't blow you away with mechanical depth, but for a few hours of smashing robots as Four Arms, it's exactly the kind of comfort-food gaming that still holds up.
