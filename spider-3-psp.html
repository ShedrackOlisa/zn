The Spider-Man 3 movie was a mess, but the tie-in game is a genuine guilty pleasure. Loosely built around the 2007 film's plot, you're juggling Peter Parker's personal life - his relationship with Mary Jane, his rivalry with Eddie Brock - against an open-world Manhattan full of street crime, spider tokens, and the corrupting influence of the black suit itself. The PSP port trims the draw distance and simplifies the open world compared to console versions, but the web-swinging, the morality system tracking how aggressively you use the black suit, and the boss fights against Sandman, Venom, and New Goblin all survive the compression well.

The Black Suit Turns Combat Into Glorious Overkill

Once the black suit shows up, combat shifts from acrobatic and clean to brutal and aggressive - tendril attacks that throw cars, ground-slam finishers, and a noticeably meaner moveset overall. It's completely over the top in the best way, and the morality system tracking your aggression as you use it adds a small layer of consequence that keeps the power fantasy from feeling completely weightless. Switching between suits mid-playthrough genuinely changes how missions feel to play.

An Open World That's Smaller But Still Fun To Swing Through

The PSP version's Manhattan is scaled down from the console release, but the core web-swinging loop - building momentum, launching off rooftops, weaving between skyscrapers - holds up well even with the shorter draw distance. Times Square, Central Park, and the docks are all represented, and stopping random street crimes for hero points remains a satisfying, if repetitive, way to pad out play sessions between story missions.

Boss Fights Carry The Movie's Best Moments

Sandman's massive sand-creature encounters, Venom's brutal brawling style, and the aerial duels against New Goblin are all highlights, giving the campaign's roughly 8-10 hour runtime a handful of genuinely memorable set pieces. They're not the deepest boss fights in gaming, but they nail the spectacle the film itself was going for, arguably better than the movie did.

Tips Before You Jump In:
• Stick to the standard suit for most story missions - the black suit's corruption meter builds fast if you lean on it too often.
• Hold the swing button longer to build momentum before releasing; it makes traversal noticeably faster.
• Stop street crimes whenever you see them - they're the most reliable way to earn hero points for suit unlocks.
• Use the lock-on feature during boss fights; the camera can get chaotic without it.
• Save Bluetooth controller input for the New Goblin fight specifically - it's the toughest encounter and needs precise web-shooter timing.

Performance On PPSSPP

The game runs comfortably on most Android devices, holding around 30fps with flagship phones able to push higher after adjusting rendering resolution. The compressed ISO doesn't noticeably hurt the black suit's visual effects, which is the game's most striking moment visually. Load times between missions stay short, keeping momentum going during longer play sessions.

Is Spider-Man 3 Worth Playing Today?

If you're after a deep, refined superhero game, look elsewhere - but if you want a big, dumb, entertaining romp through an open-world Manhattan with an overpowered black suit, this delivers exactly that. It doesn't take itself too seriously, and that's precisely why it works. For fans of the era's movie tie-in games, this is one of the more replayable options on PPSSPP.
