WWII shooters on PSP are a real mixed bag, and I went into Road to Victory bracing for a rushed cash-in riding on the Call of Duty name. It's not reinventing anything, but it captures that classic, pre-Modern Warfare Call of Duty feeling of historical, boots-on-the-ground combat better than a lot of full-priced console shooters from the same era managed.

Road to Victory follows the Pacific and European theaters of World War II across a series of missions that hit the genre's greatest hits - storming beaches under fire, clearing bunkers with a squad, picking off enemies from a sniper's nest. A Campaign That Actually Mixes Up Its Battles

Rather than sticking to one front, the campaign moves between iconic WWII battles including Iwo Jima and Peleliu, giving each mission a distinct visual identity and combat rhythm. Beach assaults put you under constant fire with limited cover, jungle sections slow things down into tense bunker-clearing pushes, and the occasional vehicle segment breaks up the on-foot pacing without overstaying its welcome. It's a linear, mission-based structure through and through, but the variety keeps the six-or-so-hour campaign from ever feeling like it's repeating itself.

Squad Combat And Surprisingly Solid Controls

Commanding an AI squad through bunker clearances and beach pushes gives the combat a bit more tactical texture than a pure run-and-gun shooter would have. Your squadmates actually contribute meaningfully to firefights rather than standing around, which matters a lot in a genre where AI companions are often glorified scenery. The touchscreen controls handle aiming and movement better than you'd expect from a PSP-era FPS port, though grenade throwing takes some getting used to - expect a few early missions where you lob one somewhere you didn't intend.

Multiplayer: Small Maps, Big Chaos

If you can find people to play ad-hoc with, the multiplayer here is a genuine highlight. Maps are compact and built for close-quarters chaos rather than long sightlines, which keeps every match frantic and fast-paced. Bot support means solo players aren't left without anything to shoot at, filling out matches when human opponents aren't available. It's not a deep competitive multiplayer suite by any stretch, but for quick, scrappy shootouts on the go, it does exactly what it needs to.

Sound Design That Punches Above Its Weight

For a PSP game, the audio work here is genuinely impressive - gunshots have real punch, explosions rumble with more weight than the hardware should reasonably allow, and ambient battle noise does a lot of heavy lifting in selling the chaos of each historical battlefield. It's a small detail, but it's one of the things that makes Road to Victory feel like a proper Call of Duty entry rather than a licensed knockoff riding on the name alone.

A Few Things I'd Tell A Friend Before They Download:
• Practice grenade throws in an early, low-stakes mission before you actually need them under fire.
• Let your AI squad take point in bunker-clearing sections; they're more useful than most PSP-era companions.
• Try ad-hoc multiplayer with a friend if you can - it's a genuine highlight most players skip.
• Use headphones if possible; the sound design deserves better than a phone's tiny speaker.
• Treat this as a shorter, tighter CoD campaign rather than expecting console-length scope.

Performance And The Honest Limitations

This runs smoothly on virtually any modern Android device through PPSSPP, with no meaningful slowdown even during the busier beach-landing sequences. That said, it's very much a product of the pre-Modern Warfare CoD era - animations are stiff, the story is thin scaffolding between action set pieces, and outside of scripted encounters the enemy AI isn't particularly clever. Bot-filled multiplayer, while fun, is clearly a stand-in for a real player base that no longer exists. None of that stops it from being an enjoyable handheld shooter, but it's worth knowing what generation of game you're downloading.

Is Call Of Duty: Road To Victory Worth Downloading?

If you have any nostalgia for the days when Call of Duty meant historical battlefields instead of futuristic warfare, this is a nostalgia trip well worth taking. It's not going to compete with Modern Warfare or even the console Black Ops entries, but as a compact, well-paced handheld WWII shooter, it holds up far better than most licensed portable spin-offs from its era. For a quick, satisfying dose of boots-on-the-ground combat, it's an easy recommendation.
