Playing the villains changes everything

Most Naruto games put you in Team 7's shoes; this one hands you Itachi, Kisame, Deidara, Sasori, and the rest of the Akatsuki roster instead, tasking you with hunting down jinchuriki across a string of dungeon missions. It reframes familiar story beats from the opposite side, and each character's jutsu translates well into the top-down format - Deidara's clay bombs and Sasori's puppet strikes both feel distinct even without the 3D arena presentation the mainline fighters use.

Dungeon crawling over arena fighting

Rather than one-on-one duels, missions send you through maze-like dungeons packed with enemy squads, environmental traps, and boss encounters at the end. Combat is real-time and combo-based but simpler than a dedicated fighting game, leaning more on positioning and jutsu cooldowns than combo strings. It's a slower burn than Ultimate Ninja Heroes or Storm, but the dungeon-crawler pacing gives missions a sense of progression that arena fighters don't really have.

Light RPG systems add just enough depth

Between missions you'll pick up equipment and currency to improve your characters, giving the game a loot loop that's uncommon for a Naruto title. It's not deep by RPG standards, but upgrading a character's chakra pool or unlocking a new jutsu slot gives missions a sense of forward momentum that a straight arcade ladder would lack. It's the kind of system that rewards playing through multiple sessions rather than one-and-done runs.

Where it falls short of the mainline games

Be honest with yourself about what this is: a budget-scale spinoff, not a flagship Naruto fighter. The visuals are simpler, missions can feel repetitive if you binge several in a row, and the top-down perspective means you lose the spectacle of full jutsu animations that make Storm and Ultimate Ninja Heroes so flashy. If you go in expecting a AAA-tier anime fighter, you'll be disappointed; if you go in wanting a different, more grounded angle on the franchise, it delivers.

Things I Wish Someone Had Told Me Earlier:
• Upgrade chakra capacity before attack power early on - running out of jutsu mid-dungeon is the bigger problem.
• Deidara and Sasori excel at range; keep distance rather than brawling melee enemies directly.
• Explore dungeon side-paths fully - equipment drops there matter more than rushing to the boss.
• Boss encounters have exploitable patterns; dying once to learn them is faster than guessing blind.
• Don't binge too many missions back-to-back - the repetition is more noticeable in long sessions.
• Save before boss rooms; the game doesn't always checkpoint generously beforehand.

Is Legends: Akatsuki Rising Worth Playing?

If you're curious about a Naruto game that lets you actually be the Akatsuki, yes - it's a genuinely different take on the license that the mainline fighters never attempt. The dungeon-crawler pacing and light RPG progression won't appeal to everyone, and it's clearly a smaller-budget spinoff compared to Storm or Ultimate Ninja Heroes. But at around 500MB and with a perspective this unusual for the franchise, it's worth a download for anyone who wants something other than another arena fighter with the same cast of heroes.
