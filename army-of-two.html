Salem And Rios: The Real Reason This Game Works

Strip away the guns and explosions and Army of Two: The 40th Day is fundamentally a story about two guys who trust each other completely in a city that's trying to kill them. The banter is constant - sarcastic, occasionally crude, but never annoying - and it's what carries you through missions that would otherwise feel like generic shooting galleries. Playing solo, the AI-controlled Rios pulls his weight far better than I expected from a PSP-era companion bot; he flanks, he revives, and he actually reacts to the chaos around him instead of standing in a doorway waiting for scripted triggers.

Back To Back: The Mechanic That Sells The Whole Game

The signature "Back to Back" mode - where Salem and Rios stand shoulder to shoulder and mow down enemies in a slow-motion 360-degree spray - is the moment this game earns its identity. It's not subtle, it's not tactical, and it doesn't need to be. Aggro management also plays a bigger role than you'd expect for a shooter this arcade-leaning: drawing enemy attention onto yourself so your partner can flank from cover is a real tactic here, not just flavor text, and it makes even the mid-game shootouts feel like you're actually coordinating rather than just taking turns pulling triggers.

Shanghai Falling Apart Around You

The 40th Day trades the original's globe-trotting for a single collapsing city, and that focus works in its favor. Buildings crumble mid-firefight, streets flood, and the destructible-environment gimmick from the first game gets used more purposefully here as cover routes change while you're actively being shot at. It's not a huge, sprawling PSP game - there's no open map to wander - but the linear mission structure keeps the pacing tight, and the compressed ISO still carries over every scripted collapse and cinematic gunfight the console original had, just rendered at a scale the PSP hardware can actually push.

Weapon Customization That's More Toy Box Than Loadout Screen

The gunsmith system lets you slap gold plating, mismatched scopes, and absurd attachments onto everything in your arsenal, and it's honestly one of the funnest parts of the game even though none of it matters mechanically. There's a real joy in running into a warzone with a rifle that looks like it belongs in a rap video. Morality choices sprinkled through the missions - who to spare, who to shoot, what to loot - don't drastically branch the story, but they're memorable enough, and occasionally dark enough, to give a normally straightforward campaign a bit of unexpected weight.

A Few Things I'd Tell A Friend Before They Download:
• Run it at 2x resolution - explosion-heavy sequences can chug on lower-end phones otherwise.
• Don't expect deep storytelling; this is about chaos and co-op chemistry, not plot twists.
• The AI partner is genuinely competent solo, so don't feel like you need a second player to enjoy it.
• Lean into "Back to Back" mode whenever it's available - it's the game's best trick.
• Keep a save before big morality choices if you want to see how outcomes change.

Performance And The Honest Limitations

On most modern Android devices this runs smoothly at 2x internal resolution, with only occasional frame dips during the biggest explosion set pieces or when the screen fills with debris. Texture pop-in is noticeable if you're used to modern shooters, and the campaign's linearity means there's very little reason to replay missions beyond chasing morality-choice variety. It also never quite escapes its PSP-era jankiness - animations can look stiff up close, and enemy AI outside of scripted encounters is fairly basic. None of that ruins the experience, but go in knowing this is a product of its generation, not a hidden AAA masterpiece.

Is Army Of Two: The 40th Day Worth Downloading?

If you want a shooter with actual personality - one where the co-op partner banters instead of barking orders, and where the action leans into spectacle rather than realism - this is a genuinely satisfying way to spend six hours. It's not deep, it's not long, and it's rough around the edges in places, but it knows exactly what it wants to be: a loud, funny, destructible buddy shooter you can knock out in a weekend. For PSP shooter fans who never gave it a shot, it's absolutely worth the download.
