Classic Kratos Combat, Tuned For A Handheld

The Blades of Chaos remain the star of the show, chaining enemies into brutal combo strings with the same rhythm the console games are known for. Chains of Olympus adds the Arms of Sparta, a shield-and-spear combo suited to defensive play and ranged pokes, alongside magic like the Efreet for clearing crowds and Charon's Wrath for draining enemy health. Everything here translates to touchscreen reasonably well, but the quick-time events genuinely benefit from physical buttons, so a Bluetooth controller is worth the setup if you want the combat to feel as sharp as it's meant to.

An Absurd Amount Of Scale For A PSP Disc

It's still hard to believe how much spectacle Ready at Dawn packed onto PSP hardware. The opening Basilisk fight has you running along a collapsing bridge before climbing the creature itself to finish it off, and the Temple of Persephone unfolds into a sprawling dungeon far bigger than the format should reasonably allow. Boss encounters against the Basilisk, the Persian King, Charon and Persephone all carry real scale and distinct mechanical hooks rather than feeling like reused assets. On PPSSPP with resolution upscaling, these set-pieces hold up better than most games from this generation have any right to.

Puzzles That Complement The Combat

Between fights, Chains of Olympus breaks up the pacing with environmental puzzles, rotating ancient mechanisms to redirect light beams, shifting blocks to reach new platforms, and using magic abilities to reveal hidden paths. None of it approaches the complexity of the numbered console entries, but it's satisfying rather than filler, and hidden chests scattered through each level reward exploration with experience orbs and health upgrades. The linear level design keeps you moving forward while still leaving room to poke into corners if you want the extra progression.

Bonus Content And Why The Length Works In Its Favor

Once the 6-to-8-hour campaign wraps, there's a Challenge mode for testing combat skills under time pressure, unlockable costumes for replay value, and a New Game Plus that carries your upgrades into a second run. That shorter runtime compared to the mainline games actually works in this ISO's favor for mobile play, it's substantial enough to feel like a real God of War experience without demanding the kind of long-haul commitment that's harder to give on a phone. It respects your time while still delivering a complete arc.

A Few Things I'd Tell A Friend Before They Download:
• Upgrade the Blades of Chaos first, you'll rely on them for the vast majority of the campaign.
• Save the Efreet magic for large enemy groups, it clears crowds far more efficiently than melee alone.
• Don't skip quick-time events, they hand out bonus experience orbs on top of the spectacle.
• Explore every level fully, hidden chests with health and magic upgrades are easy to miss on a first pass.
• Pair a Bluetooth controller if you can, the QTEs are noticeably more forgiving with physical buttons.

Is Chains Of Olympus Worth Downloading?

This is a genuine God of War experience that just happens to be sized for a handheld, not a watered-down spin-off riding on the name. The combat is as brutal and satisfying as the mainline games, the boss fights punch well above what PSP hardware should have allowed, and the shorter runtime makes it one of the more approachable entries for a first-time player or anyone wanting a complete arc without a long commitment. On PPSSPP with a controller and a bit of resolution scaling, it holds up as one of the better action games you can carry in your pocket, essential for series fans, and a great starting point for newcomers.
