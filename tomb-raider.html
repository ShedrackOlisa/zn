Lara's Grapple Hook Changes Everything

The single biggest improvement Legend brought to the series is the grapple hook, and it's the star of this portable version too. Instead of just running and jumping between fixed platforms, you can hook onto anchor points to swing across chasms, yank loose pillars to create new paths, or rappel down cliff faces on the fly. It turns every large room into a small traversal puzzle rather than a straight corridor, and the sense of momentum when you chain a swing into a wall-run into a ledge grab is genuinely satisfying, even scaled down for a handheld screen.

A Globe-Trotting Hunt With An Actual Mystery

The Excalibur fragment hunt gives Legend a narrative spine that earlier Tomb Raider games mostly skipped in favor of "raid ancient tomb, find artifact, repeat." Here, each location peels back another layer of what happened to Lara's mother, and the story actually pays off by the credits instead of trailing off. It's not a masterpiece of writing, but it gives you a reason to care about reaching the next location beyond just wanting to see a new set of ruins, and the PSP port preserves every story beat and cutscene from the console release.

Puzzle Design That Respects Your Intelligence

Legend's puzzles lean on physics and observation rather than trial-and-error backtracking, which was a real shift for the series at the time. Levers, counterweights, light-reflecting mirrors, and pressure switches show up constantly, but they're woven into the environment rather than dumped on you as an obvious puzzle box. The Peru and Japan levels in particular strike a great balance between combat, platforming, and brain-teasers, never letting any single element overstay its welcome before moving to the next.

Tips Before You Start Exploring:
• Use Lara's binoculars to scan a room before moving, grapple points and interactive objects are often highlighted and easy to miss otherwise.
• Save often, especially before big traversal sequences, a mistimed jump late in a swing chain can cost you a lot of progress.
• The motorcycle sections control differently from the platforming, give yourself a level to adjust before judging them too harshly.
• Combat is secondary to platforming here, don't waste ammo on enemies you can just run past.
• If you get stuck on an environmental puzzle, check for grapple anchor points first, most late-game rooms hide the solution in something swingable.

Performance On PPSSPP

This is one of the smoother-running PSP-era ports on the emulator. Animation quality holds up well with buffered rendering and hardware transform enabled, and load times between areas are quick even on mid-range Android hardware. There's the occasional texture pop on a fresh area load, but nothing that gets in the way of platforming or puzzle sequences, and audio, including the game's genuinely solid voice work, comes through clean.

Is Tomb Raider: Legend Worth Downloading?

For anyone who wants to see where the modern Tomb Raider identity actually started, yes, without hesitation. It's shorter and more linear than the classic PS1-era games, but that's a deliberate trade for tighter pacing and a story that doesn't overstay its welcome. The grapple hook alone makes the traversal more interesting than most PSP-era platformers, and the puzzle design still holds up nearly two decades later. If you've got a few evenings free and want a well-paced adventure that respects your time, this is an easy recommendation.
