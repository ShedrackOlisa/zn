Switching Between The Prince And The Dark Prince Never Gets Old

The core gimmick here is genuinely clever - the Prince fights fast and acrobatic with his usual moveset, while transforming into the Dark Prince trades speed for a slower, heavier sickle-and-chain combat style with its own combos entirely. Mid-fight, you're constantly deciding which persona suits the enemies in front of you, and the added swordplay challenge rooms in this edition specifically test how fluidly you can transition between the two under pressure.

Chariot Chases Are Still Some Of The Series' Best Set Pieces

The horse-drawn chariot sequences racing through Babylon's streets remain a highlight, demanding sharp reaction time as you dodge obstacles and enemy riders at speed. They translate to PPSSPP without losing any of their tension, and honestly hold up better than a lot of vehicle sections from games twice this one's budget.

Puzzles Built Around Using Both Personas Together

Most of the environmental puzzles in Rival Swords expect you to use both the Prince's acrobatics and the Dark Prince's raw strength to progress - swinging across a gap as one, then smashing through a wall as the other. It's a smart way to justify the dual-persona mechanic outside of combat, and the new wall-run trial stages exclusive to this version push that idea even further.

Tips Before You Jump In:
• Save Dark Prince transformations for tougher enemy groups - his heavier attacks clear crowds faster than the standard Prince moveset.
• During chariot sequences, watch the road ahead rather than nearby obstacles - most crashes come from over-focusing on what's right in front of you.
• Try the extra swordplay challenge rooms early; they're a great way to get comfortable with switching combat styles before the story demands it.
• Use 2x PSP rendering resolution in PPSSPP for noticeably crisper chariot chase visuals without hurting frame rate.

Is Prince Of Persia Rival Swords Worth Your Time?
