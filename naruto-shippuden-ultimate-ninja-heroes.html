Ultimate Support Jutsu

Support Jutsu is the mechanic that defines it

Where the sequel expanded the roster and polish, this first entry introduced the idea that made the sub-series interesting in the first place: calling in a support character for a combined ultimate attack. Landing Naruto's Rasengan followed immediately by a support character's finishing blow feels great even now, and the timing window is forgiving enough that new players can pull it off without hours of practice. It's simple, but it's the kind of simple that's satisfying every single time.

Story mode covers the earliest Shippuden beats

The cutscenes here retell the timeskip's opening stretch - Naruto's return, the reunion with Sakura and Kakashi, and the earliest confrontations with Akatsuki scouts. It's shorter and less ambitious than later entries' story modes, but the voice-acted cutscenes give context to fights instead of just dropping you into an arcade ladder, and completionists will appreciate how tightly the unlockable roster ties into progressing the story.

Combat that favors speed over depth

Matches here move fast - health bars drop quickly, ultimate jutsu animations are shorter than in later entries, and there's less emphasis on chip damage or defensive meters. This makes for a game that's easy to pick up for a two-minute session but doesn't hold up as a long-term competitive fighter. If you're after a deep combo system, this isn't it; if you want quick, flashy Naruto battles you can knock out between other things, it's close to ideal.

Where the age is most visible

Being the first game of its style, some rough edges show - hit detection is looser than in the sequel, the character roster is noticeably smaller, and a few support jutsu combinations are clearly stronger than others in a way the sequel later balanced out. None of it is a dealbreaker, but going in expecting the more refined version found in Ultimate Ninja Heroes 2 will set the wrong expectations for what this earlier entry actually offers.

Is Ultimate Ninja Heroes Worth Playing Today?

As a piece of Naruto gaming history and a genuinely fun quick-session fighter, yes. It's rougher around the edges than its sequel, and the roster feels smaller by comparison, but the Ultimate Support Jutsu system still delivers satisfying moments, and the story mode gives early Shippuden arcs real context instead of skipping straight to fights. At roughly 600MB, it's an easy download if you want to see where the sub-series started before jumping into the more polished sequel.
