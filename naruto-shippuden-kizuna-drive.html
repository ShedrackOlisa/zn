The Drive system is the whole game

Rather than switching characters for variety's sake, Kizuna Drive builds its entire combat loop around tagging partners in to extend combos and cover elemental gaps - hit a fire-weak enemy with a wind-affinity ninja, then Drive into a taijutsu specialist to finish the juggle. It rewards reading the battlefield rather than just mashing your strongest attack, and once it clicks, stringing a four-hit tag combo into an enemy's weakness feels genuinely clever rather than gimmicky.

An original story that doesn't retread the anime

Most Naruto games ask you to replay arcs you've already watched. Kizuna Drive instead invents its own antagonist and plot thread that slots into the Shippuden timeline without contradicting it, giving fights actual narrative stakes instead of foregone conclusions. It's not going to be remembered as essential Naruto lore, but as a portable game it's refreshing to play through a story where you don't already know exactly how every fight ends.

Co-op changes how the missions play

Playing solo, you control both members of your Drive pair and swap manually; bring in a second player over ad-hoc and suddenly the tag mechanic becomes a real-time coordination puzzle between two people. Missions are structured in digestible chunks - clear an objective, grab loot, move to the next - which suits both quick solo sessions and longer co-op runs where you're bouncing combo setups off a friend instead of planning them alone.

Combat depth versus the Storm series

If you're coming from Ultimate Ninja Storm, expect something scaled down mechanically - there's no elaborate combo-string system or awakening mode, just tag-based hack-and-slash combat that's easy to learn and genuinely fun in short bursts. It won't satisfy anyone looking for competitive-depth fighting mechanics, but as a co-op-focused action game built for portable sessions, it does exactly what it sets out to do without overcomplicating things.

Things I Wish Someone Had Told Me Earlier:
• Learn each character's elemental affinity before missions - matching it to enemy weaknesses is the core of the combat.
• Tag partners mid-combo, not between fights - that's where the Drive system's damage bonuses actually kick in.
• Co-op missions scale difficulty up, so don't expect solo-tuned enemy counts with a second player.
• Loot from missions feeds equipment upgrades - don't skip side objectives if you want better gear early.
• If ad-hoc connection drops mid-mission, restart from the mission select screen rather than trying to reconnect mid-fight.
• The original story's later missions spike in difficulty - grind an earlier stage if you're underleveled.

Is Naruto Shippuden: Kizuna Drive Worth Playing?

If you've got a friend to play with, absolutely - this is one of the more genuinely fun co-op experiences in the Naruto game library, and the Drive tag system gives it an identity the mainline fighters don't have. Playing solo is still enjoyable but loses some of what makes it special. At roughly 750MB it's a reasonable download, and the original story is a nice change of pace from yet another retelling of the Pain arc. Not essential, but a strong pick if co-op action is what you're after.
