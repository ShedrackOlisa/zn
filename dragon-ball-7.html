Combat That Feels Like the Anime

The combat here is pure joy. You can teleport behind opponents for punishing vanish counters, clash energy beams in quick-time showdowns that genuinely reward good timing, and trigger dramatic finishers that recreate iconic anime moments, Goku's first Super Saiyan transformation against Frieza, Gohan's Cell Games finish, all of it is in here. The first time I landed one, I actually yelled out loud. Aerial freedom is a big part of what makes this feel different from older DBZ fighters too, you're not stuck on the ground; you can chase opponents through the sky, smash them into the terrain, and charge a Super Kamehameha mid-flight while the camera pulls back to sell the scale of it.

40+ Characters and Every Transformation

The roster is genuinely stacked. Goku alone comes with base, SSJ, SSJ2, SSJ3, SSG, SSB, and UI forms. Vegeta gets the same treatment plus Majin Vegeta. Gohan has his kid, teen, Ultimate, and Beast forms. Future Trunks brings SSJ Rage, and there are deep cuts too, Tapion, Pikkon, even Yamcha with a genuinely usable Wolf Fang Fist moveset. On the villain side you get every Frieza form, Cell's perfect and super perfect stages, all three Buu variants, Beerus, Hit, and Jiren. Every character carries their signature moves faithfully, which is an impressive level of care for something built by fans rather than a studio.

Story Mode and What-If Scenarios

The story mode remixes the series' major arcs with fun what-if twists, defend Namek from Frieza's army as an unlikely hero, survive the Cell Games with a different outcome, or fight through the Tournament of Power with alternate team lineups. There's also a "Dragon Road" mode, a gauntlet of increasingly tough battles that unlocks bonus characters and costumes. The writing has a fan-made charm to it, expect a few inside jokes, but it never gets in the way of the action. Clearing everything took me most of a weekend, and it never felt like padding.

Multiplayer and Local Battles

The ad-hoc multiplayer mode works well on PPSSPP if you've got a friend nearby on the same Wi-Fi. Beam clashes get genuinely tense in a way they never do against the AI, both of you mashing to win a Kamehameha-versus-Galick-Gun standoff while your characters scream their finisher lines is peak Dragon Ball energy. There's also a training mode with visible hitboxes and frame data, a feature you rarely see in fan-made fighters, which tells you how much care went into balancing this roster.

A Few Things I'd Tell A Friend Before They Download:
• Master the vanish counter, block right before a hit lands and you'll teleport behind your opponent. It's the single most important skill for winning matches.
• Save your super meter for beam clashes specifically. Winning one deals big damage and grants a short power boost on top of it.
• Every character has a hidden "ultimate" move not listed in the standard move list, worth experimenting with different inputs during Sparking mode.
• Set PPSSPP's rendering resolution to 2x rather than 3x on mid-range phones, transformation effects are the heaviest thing this game asks of your hardware.
• Use a physical controller if you have one. The game was designed around it, and the super-move inputs are noticeably easier with real buttons.

Performance and the Honest Limitations

On a mid-range phone with buffered rendering and hardware transform enabled, this runs smoothly through almost every fight, dips only show up during the busiest multi-beam clash animations. The cel-shaded visuals hold up nicely with upscaling on, though PSP-era textures are still noticeable up close if you're used to modern mobile games. It's also worth being upfront that this is fan work, not an official release, so don't expect the polish of a studio-backed title. A couple of menu transitions feel dated, and the dialogue occasionally leans into fan-service humor that won't land for everyone.

Is Shin Budokai 7 Worth Downloading?

If you're a Dragon Ball fan, yes, easily. It captures the speed, spectacle, and spirit of the anime better than most licensed mobile games manage, and at roughly 250MB it costs you almost nothing to find out. The roster alone justifies the download, and the combat has enough depth that it won't get boring after one afternoon. It's not a AAA product and it doesn't pretend to be one, but as a highly compressed PPSSPP ISO built by people who clearly love this series, it delivers exactly what it promises. Pick Goku, go Super Saiyan for the first time, and see if you don't smile.
