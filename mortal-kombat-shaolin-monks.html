Mortal Kombat: Shaolin Monks retells the events of MK2 from the perspective of Liu Kang and Kung Lao as they fight through Outworld to stop Shao Kahn's invasion. It plays nothing like a standard MK title - think 3D beat-'em-up with fixed camera angles, RPG-lite upgrades, environmental puzzles, and combat that rewards juggling enemies into hazards rather than memorized frame-perfect strings. Combat built around combos, not frame data

Where most MK games live and die on precise inputs and matchup knowledge, Shaolin Monks just wants you to hit things in creative ways. Liu Kang chains fireballs and his bicycle kick into juggles, Kung Lao mixes hat throws with spin attacks, and both characters can toss enemies into spike pits, acid pools, or rotating blades for instant kills. It's simple to pick up and genuinely satisfying to chain together, especially once you start layering upgrades from the game's light RPG progression on top of your base moveset.

Co-op is the reason to play this

Shaolin Monks was clearly designed with two players in mind, and it shows - certain puzzles need both characters hitting switches simultaneously, bosses are tuned around two sets of hands, and there's nothing quite like watching your partner uppercut an enemy into a pit you set up. On PPSSPP this means ad-hoc multiplayer across two devices, which is more setup than modern co-op games demand, but the payoff is worth it. Solo play works fine too, it just loses some of the game's best moments.

Familiar locations, reworked as levels

The level design leans hard on nostalgia for classic MK stages: the Living Forest with its ambushing trees, Goro's trap-laden Lair building to the legendary boss fight, the spike-and-blade gauntlet of the Pit II, and the acid-and-corpse aesthetic of the Dead Pool. Each location translates surprisingly well into a linear action stage, and the compressed visuals on PPSSPP still get the atmosphere across even if the textures are softer than you remember from the console version.

Finishers that swing between brutal and silly

True to the series, Shaolin Monks keeps its fatalities intact - Liu Kang's Dragon's Breath and Kung Lao's Hat Slice are as gruesome as ever - but it also brings back Friendships, the goofy non-violent finishers the mainline games mostly abandoned. Watching Liu Kang play arcade basketball after a boss fight is a strange tonal whiplash in the best way. The inputs for both are noticeably more forgiving than mainline MK, which makes them far easier to land on a touchscreen.

Things I Wish Someone Had Told Me Earlier:
• Prioritize Liu Kang's fireball upgrade first - it's his best crowd-control tool by a wide margin.
• Environmental kills (spikes, acid, blades) do more damage than any combo you can string manually.
• Find a second device and a friend for ad-hoc co-op - it genuinely changes the game.
• The secret character Smoke is hidden in the Living Forest behind an easy-to-miss path.
• If performance dips during big fireball effects, drop rendering resolution to 1x PSP.
• Juggling into environmental hazards is faster and safer than chasing full combo strings.

Is Shaolin Monks Worth Playing Today?

Absolutely, and arguably more than most of the mainline entries getting PSP ports right now. The combat is forgiving but satisfying, the co-op mode is a genuine standout, and revisiting classic MK locations as actual explorable levels still feels novel two decades later. The PPSSPP conversion isn't flawless - textures are softer and frame rate dips during flashy effects - but at roughly 600MB it's a small download for one of the most distinct entries the series ever produced. Bring a friend if you can; it's built for two.
