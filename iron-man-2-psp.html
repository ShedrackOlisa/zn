Superhero Action Flight
Movie tie-in games have a well-earned bad reputation, so I went into Iron Man 2 on PSP expecting a rushed cash-grab timed to the film's release. What I actually found was a surprisingly competent arcade flight-and-shooter that doesn't even bother retelling the movie plot beat for beat. Instead it drops you into an original storyline where you suit up as either Tony Stark or War Machine and go toe-to-toe with a roster of classic Marvel villains that never appeared on screen. It's not deep, and it's not trying to be. It's a straightforward power fantasy, and on that narrow goal, it delivers.

The whole game is built around one simple, satisfying loop: fly, target, blast, upgrade, repeat. There's no attempt at Metroidvania-style exploration or complex RPG systems, missions are self-contained action sequences where you tear through waves of enemies and a boss encounter, then bring your earnings back to upgrade your suit's weapons and armor. It's unapologetically arcade in structure, which actually works in its favor on a portable system, this is a game built for short bursts rather than marathon sessions, and it never overstays its welcome within any single mission.

Flying Actually Feels Good, Which Is The Whole Point

For a licensed PSP title, the flight model is genuinely well-tuned. Boosting through open skies, banking around obstacles, and diving toward a target all feel weighty and responsive rather than floaty or disconnected, which matters enormously for a game where flight is the primary mode of traversal in nearly every mission. Combat leans into this too, locking onto multiple enemies simultaneously and unleashing a spread of repulsor blasts across all of them at once feels appropriately powerful. It's the closest a PSP-era licensed game got to making you feel like you're actually piloting a flying weapons platform rather than just aiming a gun with extra steps.

Two Suits, Two Distinct Playstyles

Switching between Tony Stark's Iron Man armor and Don Cheadle's War Machine isn't just a cosmetic choice, the two play noticeably differently. Iron Man favors mobility and precision energy weapons, rewarding hit-and-run tactics and aerial repositioning. War Machine trades some of that agility for heavier ballistic weaponry and a tankier feel, encouraging a more head-on approach to encounters. Having both available across the campaign gives missions some welcome variety, and replaying a level with the other suit often changes the whole rhythm of the fight rather than feeling like a reskin.

Original Story, Classic Marvel Villains

Rather than rehashing scenes from the film, the game builds its own plot as a loose sequel-adjacent story, giving it room to bring in villains from Iron Man's broader comic history rather than being limited to whoever appeared in the movie. It's a smart choice that keeps the campaign from feeling like a lazy retread of a story you already know from the theater. The writing itself isn't going to win awards, cutscenes are brief and functional rather than cinematic, but it gives just enough context to justify why you're flying into the next explosive set piece.

Suit Upgrades Give Progress A Purpose

Between missions, currency earned from combat goes toward upgrading weapons, armor plating, and special abilities on both suits. The upgrade tree isn't deep, but it's satisfying in the way arcade progression should be, each unlock noticeably changes how a mission plays out, letting you clear later, tougher encounters with tools you didn't have at the start. It gives the mission-to-mission loop a sense of forward momentum that a purely score-based arcade game would lack, without bogging things down in the kind of stat-spreadsheet management that would feel out of place in a game this breezy.

Performance And The Honest Limitations

This isn't a technically demanding game by PSP standards, and it runs smoothly on PPSSPP across most modern phones without needing aggressive settings tweaks. The trade-off for that lightness is scope, mission variety is fairly limited by the mid-game, and the core loop of fly-shoot-upgrade doesn't evolve much across the campaign's length. Visuals are clearly PSP-era, functional rather than impressive, and the story is thin enough that skipping cutscenes loses you nothing important. It's a game best enjoyed for what it is: a short, arcade-y superhero romp, not a deep action experience.

Is Iron Man 2 Worth Playing In 2026?

If you want a no-fuss, arcade-style superhero power fantasy you can pick up for fifteen minutes at a time, yes, this delivers exactly that. It's not going to be remembered as a hidden gem of narrative depth, but the flight combat is genuinely well executed for a licensed tie-in, and having two distinct playable suits adds more replay value than you'd expect. At around 350MB compressed, it's a light download for a good chunk of undemanding, satisfying superhero action. Think of it as the perfect background game, something to half-focus on while a podcast plays, just enjoying the spectacle of being in the suit.
