ODM Gear: The Best Movement System In A Handheld Game

Forget simple attack buttons - here you're managing gas fuel, blade condition, anchor points and momentum all at once. Lock onto a building or a Titan's limb, fire your grapples, and swing yourself into position to hit the one spot that actually matters: the nape. It took me a couple of missions to stop over-thinking it, but once the movement clicks, chaining swing after swing without ever landing feels genuinely exhilarating, and it's rare for a game this size to nail a system this physical.

Build Your Own Scout, Then Watch The Story Bend Around You

Instead of locking you into an existing character, you create your own recruit and drop them into the 104th Training Corps alongside Eren, Mikasa, Armin and the rest of the cast. Shared meals, side conversations and joint missions raise your bond with each character, and that bond quietly reshapes dialogue and even who makes it out of certain story beats alive. It's a smart way to put you inside a story everyone already knows without breaking what made it work in the first place.

Titans Still Feel Like A Genuine Threat

The horror underneath the spectacle is what keeps this from feeling like a button-mashing anime cash-in. A mistimed swing can leave your Scout dangling in a Titan's grip while your squad screams your name in the background, and that tension doesn't let up even after dozens of hours. The compressed ISO holds up well when a wall gets swarmed by a dozen-plus Titans at once, which is more than some full-sized console ports manage.

Tips Before You Jump In:
• Use a Bluetooth controller if you have one - the dual-stick anchor aiming is far more precise than touch controls.
• Set "Rendering Resolution" to 2x PSP in PPSSPP for noticeably sharper Titan models without hurting frame rate.
• Prioritize the affinity conversations early - the bonuses they unlock make the mid-game Titan encounters much more manageable.
• Keep an eye on gas fuel during long swings; running dry mid-air is the fastest way to end up in a Titan's fist.

Performance On PPSSPP

This is one of the smoother anime adaptations you'll run on the emulator. It holds a steady 60fps through most encounters, with the occasional dip only showing up when a full squad and a swarm of Titans are on screen together. The compressed ISO trims file size without gutting the animation work, so every kill, every grapple, and every near-miss still lands with the impact it's supposed to.

Is Attack On Titan 2 Worth Your Time?

If you're a fan of the anime, this is close to essential - it lets you live inside the story rather than just watch it. If you've never seen a single episode but enjoy fast, physical action games, the ODM movement alone is worth the download. Either way, this is one of the best Attack on Titan experiences you can get outside of a console, and for some players, it might end up more memorable than the source material itself.
