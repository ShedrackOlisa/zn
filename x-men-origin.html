Regeneration As A Combat Mechanic, Not Just A Gimmick

Most games would treat Wolverine's healing factor as a passive stat boost. This one builds entire combat encounters around it: you can shrug off attacks that would kill any other character, keep pressing forward through damage that visibly tears at your body, and watch the wounds close as your health regenerates mid-fight. It fundamentally changes how you approach combat, aggression is rewarded rather than punished, because retreating to nurse a health bar is rarely necessary. It's the single mechanic that makes this feel like an actual Wolverine game rather than a generic action title with his skin slapped on.

Claw Combat That Doesn't Pull Punches

The combo system is straightforward but satisfying, chain light and heavy claw strikes into finishing moves that dismember and impale with a level of violence that's genuinely surprising for a movie tie-in. Environmental interaction adds extra flair, throwing enemies into hazards or using destructible cover as a weapon, and the pace rarely slows down long enough to get repetitive across the campaign's length.

Unlockable Costumes And A Varied Campaign

Beyond the main story, there's a decent unlockable costume roster to work toward, including classic comic-accurate looks that diverge from the movie's muted color palette. The level design takes you through genuinely different settings, jungle ambushes, snowbound military facilities, and lab environments tied to the Weapon X program, which keeps the hack-and-slash loop from feeling monotonous over the course of the campaign.
