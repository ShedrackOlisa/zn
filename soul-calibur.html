Weapon-Based Combat Still Feels Unlike Any Other Fighter

Every character wields a distinct weapon type with its own range, speed, and risk profile, and that variety changes the entire feel of a match depending on matchup. Nightmare's massive Soul Edge trades speed for devastating range and damage, while Ivy's whip-sword hybrid demands you think about spacing in ways most fighting games never ask of you. Landing a just-frame parry or a precisely-timed guard impact still feels as satisfying here as it did on console, and the PSP port hasn't sanded down any of that precision.

Kratos Is A Genuinely Great Guest Character

Guest fighters in crossover titles are usually gimmicky at best, but Kratos slots into SoulCalibur's combat system with surprising elegance - his Blades of Chaos play like a legitimate weapon archetype rather than a reskin, and his special moves reference God of War without feeling out of place next to Mitsurugi's katana or Siegfried's greatsword. It's one of the better examples of a crossover character actually being designed with care.

Tales Of Souls Mode Is A Genuinely Good Single-Player Campaign

Rather than a boring gauntlet of straight fights, Tales of Souls dresses up its single-player content with light narrative framing and character-specific paths through the roster, giving you a real reason to learn multiple fighters instead of sticking with one.
