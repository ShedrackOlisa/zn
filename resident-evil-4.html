Combat That Still Feels Tense Two Decades Later

Standing still to aim sounds like it should feel dated, but it's exactly what makes RE4's combat so deliberate and tense - you have to read each Ganado's movement and pick your shot before they close the distance. Environmental kills are still satisfying too; shoot a hanging lantern and burn out an entire cluster of enemies at once. The boss encounters remain genuinely memorable, from the lake-dwelling Del Lago to the towering, blind Garrador that hunts by sound alone. None of that tension gets lost in the port to PPSSPP.

The Merchant Is Still One Of Gaming's Best Side Characters

Tucked into hidden corners of the map, the Merchant buys your collected treasures and sells weapon upgrades, healing items, and maps, all delivered with that iconic gravelly voice. The upgrade paths are genuinely meaningful choices - the Red9 remains the best all-around pistol, the Striker the best value shotgun, and deciding where to sink your hard-earned pesetas is half the fun of the mid-game.

The Ashley Escort Sections Hold Up Better Than Their Reputation

RE4's escort mission gets a bad rap in gaming discourse, but playing it again here reminds you it was actually well-designed - Ashley can hide in dumpsters, follow or wait on command, and while she's vulnerable, she's rarely the frustrating dead weight people remember. The infamous water room section is still a genuine pain, but that's part of the game's identity at this point.

Tips Before You Jump In:
• Save your pesetas for the Red9 pistol early - it's the single best value upgrade purchase in the whole game.
• Shoot Ganados in the legs to drop them, then follow up with a knife finisher to save ammo.
• Upgrade the Striker shotgun's capacity first; the free ammo bonus pays for itself fast.
• Use a Bluetooth controller if you can - precise aiming is much harder on touch controls during crowd fights.
• Play with headphones if you want the full effect; the Regenerators' breathing is genuinely unsettling in the dark.

Performance On PPSSPP

This runs well across most Android devices, holding a steady 30fps with flagship phones able to push 60fps after lowering a few settings. The compressed ISO doesn't visibly hurt lighting or particle effects, and the rain during the waterfall sequence still looks solid. Load times between areas are quick, keeping the pacing tight even on mid-range hardware.

Is Resident Evil 4 Worth Playing Today?

Yes, without much hesitation. It's one of the best games ever made, and this compressed ISO doesn't ask you to sacrifice much to play it on a phone. The graphics are dated by modern standards and touch controls take some adjustment, but with a controller and a bit of patience, this is still one of the most replayable survival-horror experiences around. Whether you're revisiting it or playing for the first time, it earns its reputation.
