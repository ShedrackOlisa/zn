God Hand is Capcom's forgotten masterpiece - a game where you build your own combo strings from scratch, dodge hits by physically weaving your character out of the way, and humiliate bosses by slapping them around. God Hand is a beat 'em up built around one idea: your move list belongs to you. Instead of handing you a fixed combo string, the game gives you a menu of over a hundred attacks and lets you bolt them onto the four face buttons in whatever order you like. Want a jab, jab, elbow, then a spinning kick that launches an enemy across the street? Build it. Want three heavy punches that leave you wide open but end fights in seconds? Combat that rewards patience

The dodge is the heart of it. Flicking the stick makes Gene duck, weave, or backstep, and every dodge is fast enough to slip a punch but short enough that spamming it gets you hit. Learning to read wind-ups is the whole skill curve. Once it clicks, crowds of four or five enemies stop feeling like a mob and start feeling like a rhythm you can play.

Filling the Tension gauge unlocks the God Reel, a roulette of finishers that range from a suplex to a kick that sends a man into orbit. The God Hand itself is the panic button: activate it and Gene becomes untouchable for a few seconds, breaking guards and staggering everything in reach. Both meters drain fast, so the game constantly asks whether you cash out now or hold on for a worse moment.

A difficulty that watches you play

There is no difficulty menu doing the work here. Land hits without taking damage and the level meter climbs from 1 to 2 to 3 and finally to DIE, where enemies block more, attack in tighter groups, and punish every lazy input. Take a beating and it slides back down. The game is essentially negotiating with you in real time, and reaching DIE and surviving there is the best thing it offers.

The tone is part of the design

Enemies get knocked into signposts and bounce off them. A boss fights you in a luchador mask while disco plays. There are chihuahua races and a casino you can lose your money in between chapters. None of it undercuts the combat, because the fighting underneath is serious, precise and difficult. The jokes just keep the tension from turning sour when a stage beats you for the sixth time.

Things I Wish Someone Had Told Me Earlier:
• Learn to dodge before you learn to attack - the weave is your best friend, not your combo string.
• Don't get greedy with long combos; enemies punish overcommitment hard, especially in groups.
• Save your God Reel finishers for tough bosses rather than burning them on regular mooks.
• Taking a hit isn't always bad - the game's tension system rewards aggression even when you're on the back foot.
• Use a controller if you have one. The stick-based dodge fights back against touch controls.

Who it is for

If you like action games that make you earn every stage, this one is worth the frustration. The early chapters are the hardest part of the learning curve, and the game gets noticeably better once you have a custom move list you trust. Play it with a controller if you can. The dodging is stick-based and touch controls will fight you on it.

Is God Hand Worth Playing Today?

Absolutely. This is one of those games whose reputation has only grown with time - divisive on release, recognized now as one of Capcom's most creative experiments. The custom combo system still feels ahead of its time, the humor hasn't aged into cringe the way a lot of mid-2000s comedy has, and on PPSSPP it runs smoothly enough that the compressed file size feels like a steal. If you're tired of safe, predictable action games, this is the antidote. Just don't be surprised if you end up yelling at your phone. Everyone does eventually.
