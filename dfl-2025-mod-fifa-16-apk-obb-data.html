The menu system has been rebuilt with a sleeker, darker theme that looks like it belongs on a current-generation console rather than bolted onto a decade-old mobile engine. It's the kind of polish that tells you the people behind this actually care about football, not just about slapping new numbers on old code.

Gameplay Tweaks That Actually Matter

The core FIFA 16 engine hasn't been touched at its foundation - it's still the same responsive, weighty passing and shooting system longtime fans love - but the AI and pacing have been noticeably adjusted. Defenders track runs far more reliably than they did on release, goalkeepers parry shots realistically instead of catching everything, and through-ball effectiveness has been rebalanced so it's useful without being a guaranteed goal machine every time. These aren't flashy changes, but they're exactly the kind of tuning that makes an old engine feel current again.
