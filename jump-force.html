The Roster: Smaller Than Console, Still Stacked

This portable version trims the roster down from the console original's 40-plus down to a still-respectable 20-plus, and the trimming is sensible rather than random, the heavy hitters everyone actually wants are here. Goku and Vegeta bring their Super Saiyan forms, Naruto and Sasuke arrive with their strongest late-series abilities, Luffy shows up in Gear Fourth, and Ichigo, Gon, Killua, Yugi, and Kenshiro round things out with genre-spanning variety. Each character keeps their signature move intact, watching a Kamehameha collide with a Rasenshuriken in the same arena is exactly the kind of dream matchup this game exists to deliver.

Combat: Simple Inputs, Maximum Spectacle

Don't come in expecting Tekken-level technical depth, that's not the design goal here. Light attack, heavy attack, block, dash, and a handful of special move inputs cover the entire combat system, making it accessible for anyone regardless of fighting game experience. What it lacks in mechanical complexity it makes up for in presentation, arenas take dynamic damage as characters get launched through buildings, ultimate moves fill the screen with genuinely flashy animation, and combos feel satisfying to land even without deep execution requirements. It's a game about spectacle first, and on that metric, the PSP conversion holds up remarkably well.

Story Mode: Ridiculous In The Best Way

The original story campaign, built around your custom character teaming up with the main cast against a reality-merging villain, is intact here, cheesy dialogue and all. Missions are fairly repetitive structurally, beat up waves of enemies, rescue an ally, fight a boss, but the fan-service payoff of watching Goku and Luffy fight side by side against a shared threat carries it through the repetition. It's not going to be remembered for its writing, the dialogue reads like enthusiastic fan fiction, but that's clearly not what anyone downloading this game is here for.

Custom Character: Build Your Own Anime Hero

One of the more genuinely creative features carried over is the custom character system, letting you build an original avatar and equip moves borrowed from across the entire roster's series. Want a character who throws a Kamehameha, follows up with a Rasengan, and finishes with a Gum-Gum Pistol? That's entirely possible here, even if the customization options are somewhat trimmed compared to console. For a fan of crossover fiction, building a personal dream character and fighting alongside the actual anime cast is a novelty that doesn't wear off quickly, even with the PSP version's reduced options.

The Honest Limitations Of A Fan Conversion

Let's be clear-eyed about what this is: a fan-made PSP conversion, not an official portable release. Graphics take a real downgrade from console, frame rate can dip during the busiest ultimate move animations, and some voice lines sound noticeably compressed. The story mode's repetitive mission structure wears thin faster than the roster's novelty does. None of this is surprising given the PSP was never intended to run a modern 3D fighting game, and the fact that this runs at all, with most of the character roster and move sets intact, is honestly more impressive than the rough edges are disappointing.

Is Jump Force Worth Playing In 2026?
